# Waga — 3-minute hackathon pitch

Open `pitch/index.html` in a browser. Advance with → or space.

---

### Slide 1 — Title (~10s)
“We’re **Waga** — AI-assisted food affordability monitoring for Ethiopia. Built so cash programmes can act on this week’s markets, not last month’s.”

### Slide 2 — Problem (~25s)
“Ethiopia’s food prices move weekly. The official data that describes them moves monthly.  
An NGO officer setting cash for tens of thousands of households still works from a Joint Market Monitoring factsheet that is already stale. She knows it — so she guesses from WhatsApp, adds a margin, and signs.”

### Slide 3 — Stakes (~30s)
“In our Addis data, a five-staple basket jumped **18.9% in thirty days** — 4,142 to 4,924 birr.  
If transfer values sit on last month’s number, you’re off by **781 birr per household**. Across 50,000 households that’s **39 million birr in one month**.  
Families underfed, or donor money wasted. Both are failures.”

### Slide 4 — Solution (~25s)
“**Waga** turns verified market prices into a decision you can defend in writing.  
Four pieces: a scored Telegram agent network, a 72-hour affordability score, a named-market heat map, and an AI humanitarian copilot.”

### Slide 5 — How it works (~25s)
“Agents in Merkato, Ehil Berenda, Shola — speak or type in Amharic, Afaan Oromo, or English.  
Humans review every price. We publish a cell only with at least three accepted submissions.  
Then the dashboard and copilot answer: *how should we adjust cash assistance?*”

### Slide 6 — Why AI (~30s)
“Governing rule: **AI never invents a price.** Every figure cites a published index cell.  
What’s live: Amharic and Oromo speech-to-text in Telegram, with agent confirmation.  
What’s decision-ready: spike detection that doesn’t cry crisis on a rising market, weekly briefs with contribution analysis, and a copilot where rules produce numbers and the model only narrates.  
We deliberately refuse forecasting and imputation. Missing means missing.”

### Slide 7 — Built (~20s)
“This is not a mockup. Backend, review pipeline, reputation scoring, multilingual intake — shipped.  
Anyone can copy a chart. Nobody casually copies a trusted agent network in Merkato.”

### Slide 8 — Why us (~20s)
“We don’t compete with JMMI’s national coverage. We close the gap it structurally can’t: **72 hours, named markets, full audit trail, zero imputation.**  
The real incumbent is Excel plus guesswork. That’s what we beat.”

### Slide 9 — Why now (~15s)
“Food inflation is back at 15%. Humanitarian budgets collapsed. Coordination is thinner.  
Fewer analysts need cheaper, faster, auditable signal. We break even on roughly **four NGO customers**.”

### Slide 10 — Close (~15s)
“Mission: make the real cost of food visible fast enough to act on.  
We’ll demo the loop end-to-end. Next step: three NGO pilots on a live transfer-value decision.  
**Waga** — Ethiopia’s price layer for the week you’re deciding, not the month that already passed.”

## Judge FAQ (keep short)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is this just ChatGPT on prices? | No. Numbers come from reviewed index cells. AI narrates and cites; inventing a price is a bug. |
| Why not use free JMMI/WFP data? | Free and monthly. We sell speed, market granularity, and a decision layer. Complementary, not a clone. |
| What’s the moat? | The agent network + reputation + human review. Software is copyable; Merkato trust isn’t. |
| Scope today? | Addis, 9 markets, 5 staples. Honest limits beat overclaiming. |
| Where’s Cursor? | Built the stack and contracts with Cursor agents — shipping speed is part of the story; the product is the local data system. |
